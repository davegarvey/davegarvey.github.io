The first time I tried speaking to a computer was around 2000, in Microsoft Word. It was hopeless, and I went back to the keyboard.

This post, by contrast, was talked into existence. I dictated to an LLM, it asked me questions, and it drafted the post from my answers. A few weeks ago I wouldn't have done that. Now I barely type to LLMs at all.

<!--more-->

What triggered this was how much of modern work now revolves around LLMs. Ask about anything in depth and you get an answer with several points, so you write a long reply, and get another long answer back. I type quickly, but after a while it's a slog. There's a reason we jump on a Zoom call to talk rather than trade long messages in Slack.

What I didn't expect was the effect on how I talk. Dictation is a stream. Mid-sentence, I can't say "go back six words" or "forget that". I can tidy up afterwards, but not while I'm speaking. So I've slowed down. I'm more intentional. Rather than ramble to an LLM, I like to pause and think, which sometimes means 30 seconds of silence. Thankfully the LLM doesn't seem to find it awkward.

A quiet place to work helps. Dictating in a noisy café may not go so well, and in an office I'd worry about distracting people.

Having the LLM talk back is another matter. For technical work I want something I can reread, so the arrangement suits me: I talk, it writes.

I never thought I'd become a dictator, but here we are.
